# Song check report, in plain words

*Updated 17 Nov 2026. No music knowledge needed to read this.*

## 1. What we did
We took **6 school lessons** (red blood cells, heat, motion, trigonometry, fractions and waves) and turned each one into a **song** using an AI music tool.

For the last 4 lessons we made each song in **10 different music styles**, and each style twice, **once with a man singing and once with a woman singing**. That's **20 versions of every song**.

Then a computer checked every version, to make sure the singer sang **every word correctly** and didn't **change any fact**.

## 2. How a song is checked (in 3 steps)
1. **Take the music away.** A tool removes the instruments, so only the singing voice is left.
2. **Write down what was sung.** Two different "listening" programs write out the words they hear. They listen **4 times**, so one bad listen doesn't count.
3. **Compare with our lyric sheet.** A mistake only counts if **most of the listens agree** on it. For unclear spots, we cut out those few seconds and listen **10 more times**. We also read the written-out words ourselves and tick off a list of "must-hear" phrases (like "speed of light is c").

## 3. The four grades
| Grade | What it means | Do we use it? |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Good** | Every fact is sung correctly. Small slips like a missing "the" are fine. | Yes |
| 👂 **Listen first** | Probably fine, but one spot is unclear. A person should listen at the time given. | Yes, after a quick listen |
| 🔁 **Failed, remade** | A fact was missing or wrong, so we made it again with clearer instructions. We try **2 more times** at most. | Only if the remake is good |
| ⛔ **Dropped** | Failed 3 times in a row. We stop, to avoid wasting money. | No |

**Simple rule of thumb:**

- **Safe picks:** Indie, Bhangra, Afrobeats, Pop-punk, Funk.
- **Fine with a little extra budget:** Reggaeton, Jazz, A cappella (man's voice only).
- **Risky:** K-pop, DnB.

**Man vs woman singer:** the woman's versions failed more often on the first try (15 failures vs 10 for the man's), and **5 of the 6 dropped versions were women's**. If only one voice is needed in a risky style, pick the man's.

## 6. What we learned (in plain words)
*This is section 8 of the full report, "What the QC taught us", rewritten simply.*
These lessons are now built into how every new song is made.

1. **Too many words per second makes the singer skip and mumble.**
   In our second song (heat), we squeezed too many words into 90 seconds and **7 out of 11 styles failed**. When we cut the words down so the singer has time to breathe, **9 out of 10 styles were usable**.\
   → *Now:* every lyric sheet is written slowly enough that every style can sing it comfortably.

2. **Very short words and single letters get swallowed.**
   One-word answers like "Heat!" and single letters like "T", "v" and "c" were often skipped or changed. One version sang "V" instead of "c" for the speed of light.\
   → *Now:* we repeat short answers ("Heat, heat!") and tell the singer exactly how to say each letter ("tee", "vee", "see").

3. **Long science words at the end of a fast line get mumbled.**
   "Trigonometric identities" came out garbled in 4 out of 20 versions.\
   → *Now:* long science words get extra space or their own line.

4. **The computer check alone can miss mistakes.**
   Twice the singer **joined two lines into one wrong sentence** (e.g. "Air moves into arteries and veins"), and the computer score still said "good".\
   → *Now:* we also read the written-out words of every flagged version and tick off a list of must-hear phrases. This caught **5 versions that taught something wrong**.

5. **Some music styles have bad habits.** (See section 5.) DnB starts with a long music-only part, the woman's a cappella version loses words after the pause, and K-pop swaps letters.\
   → *Now:* we warn about these in the instructions. Where a long music-only start still happens, we **trim it down afterwards** (for example, the trigonometry DnB songs now go from 22 seconds of music before the singing to about 5.5 seconds).

6. **Sometimes the AI service crashes while making a song.**\
   → *Now:* we simply make it again. A crashed song isn't normally charged.
